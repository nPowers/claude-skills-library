# Statute Analysis — Rafal Fryc Method

## Description
A structured statute analysis skill that breaks down legislative text into interpretive elements: scope, operative provisions, exceptions, cross-references, and practical implications. Use it to produce annotated statute summaries and highlight interpretive issues.

## Platforms
- Claude Desktop: Supported
- Claude Code: Not Supported (analyzes provided text without code dependencies)

## Instructions
1. Ask the user to supply the statute or specific provisions to analyze, including exact article numbers and jurisdiction.
2. Segment the provision into parts (definitions, operative clauses, exceptions, penalties) and present those segments with brief labels.
3. For each segment, provide a plain-language explanation and note any legal terms that require definition or a statutory cross-reference.
4. Identify potential ambiguities, internal inconsistencies, or drafting gaps and explain how those might be interpreted by courts or regulators.
5. List related authorities or typical interpretive canons that might resolve ambiguities (textual, purposive, legislative history), clearly distinguishing between description and recommendation.
6. Produce an annotated, citation-ready summary suitable for inclusion in a memo, and propose clarifying questions the user could raise with legislative drafters or counsel.
7. Offer follow-ups: drafting amendment language, mapping case law that has interpreted the provision, or translating the provision for non-specialist stakeholders.

## Example Usage
- "Analyze Article 15 of [Act name]: split it into operative parts and note ambiguities."
- "Prepare an annotated summary of the statute regulating consumer warranties."
- "Suggest amendment language to clarify exception clauses in this provision."

## Note
This method provides interpretive assistance and drafting support but does not substitute for legal advice. Verify legal interpretations against authoritative sources and consult a licensed lawyer for high-stakes matters.
